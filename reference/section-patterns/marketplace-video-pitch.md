# Section Pattern — Solutions Marketplace Video Pitch

Three artifacts, one submission. The video carries the scored content; the deck aids the
assessors; the form is the marketplace's shop window and its compliance record.

## video-script (required)

**Purpose.** The scored deliverable. A timed spoken script covering the four required
content elements in the order the announcement states them, inside the stated length limit.

**Structure.** Timing marks against the rubric weighting, not against the writer's sense of
proportion. If Technical Merit is 45% of the score, it gets roughly 45% of the runtime.
Every claim spoken must survive being transcribed and read cold, because it will be.

**Discipline.**
- Write for the ear. Short sentences. One idea per sentence. No subordinate stacking.
- Every on-screen graphic gets a legibility note; assessors check this mechanically.
- Mark the visual alongside the words, so production has one document, not two.
- Budget to roughly 90% of the length limit. Encode and trim margin is not optional.

## slide-package (required)

**Purpose.** The prescribed chart deck. Assessors read it alongside the video; it does not
replace the video and must not contradict it.

**Structure.** Exactly the chart count the announcement prescribes, in the Government's own
template, in its order. No cover of our own, no appendix, no backup slides.

**Discipline.** Anything scored must also be said in the video. Treat the deck as the
assessor's note-taking surface, not as the argument's home.

## form-content (required)

**Purpose.** Title, keywords, abstract, and the declared field selections.

**Discipline.** The abstract is usually unscored and is nonetheless the highest-traffic text
in the submission: it decides whether a Government user presses play. The declared fields
(authorization status, business size, foreign ownership) are checked against public
registries. Write them to be true, not to be impressive.
